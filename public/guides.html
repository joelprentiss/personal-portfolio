Estate sale guides for owners and families

Practical tools for established estate businesses and the families comparing their services, including a 20-part Lean and Six Sigma operations series. Written from a builder's perspective in Georgetown, Texas.

Start with the operating playbook

Operations 01 / Start here

How to Run a More Efficient Estate Sale Business: A Practical Lean Playbook

Start by making the work visible. Choose one repeated delay or error, give it an owner, and test a change without weakening the service the family agreed to receive.

Prepare the sale

Operations 02 / Preparation

How to Reduce Estate Sale Setup Time Without Rushing the Job

Measure what interrupts setup before asking the crew to move faster. Protect preparation quality while reducing avoidable waiting and repeated handling.

Operations 03 / Preparation

An Estate Sale Photography Workflow That Prevents Duplicate Work

Give every photo assignment a clear owner and a visible finish. Connect the item, the image, and the approved listing before anyone repeats the work.

Operations 06 / Preparation

Estate Sale Inventory Systems: Keep Items, Photos, Prices and Sold Status Connected

Choose the record detail the job actually needs. Then keep every change tied to the same item or group reference from preparation through closeout.

Operations 07 / Preparation

A 5S Setup Kit for Estate Sale Crews: Stop Searching for Supplies

Give the crew’s tools a known home and a dependable restock routine. Keep this system strictly separate from decisions about the client’s belongings.

Operations 08 / Preparation

How to Price Estate Sale Items Efficiently Without Guessing

Spend research effort where uncertainty matters. Keep the evidence, the selling context, and the authority to approve a price visible.

Operations 09 / Sale-day flow

An Estate Sale Readiness Checklist: What to Verify Before the Doors Open

“Almost ready” is not a shared definition. Check the conditions that matter, name the person who can resolve each gap, and make the opening decision explicit.

Plan people and capacity

Operations 04 / People and capacity

How Many Staff Does an Estate Sale Need? Plan by Workload, Not Guesswork

There is no universal crew size. Plan the work and the coverage first, then check whether the available people have the time and skills to deliver it.

Operations 10 / People and capacity

How to Train Estate Sale Staff Without Making the Owner the Bottleneck

Teach a clear task and its limits. Staff need to know what good work looks like, what they can decide, and when the next step belongs to someone else.

Operations 11 / People and capacity

How to Schedule Multiple Estate Sales Without Overloading Your Crew

A free date on the sales calendar does not prove the work fits. Plan preparation, specialist decisions, public hours, pickup, and closeout across the same crew.

Run the sale and finish the handoffs

Operations 05 / Sale-day flow

How to Speed Up Estate Sale Checkout Without Losing Track of Sales

Keep routine transactions moving by giving questions a clear resolution path. A shorter line only helps if items, prices, payments, and releases stay connected.

Operations 12 / Sale-day flow

An Estate Sale Floor Plan That Reduces Backtracking and Bottlenecks

Follow the actual trips people make. Move the information or supplies that cause unnecessary crossings while preserving the property’s required routes and boundaries.

Operations 13 / Handoffs and closeout

How to Handle Last-Minute Estate Sale Changes Without Starting Over

Record the specific change, confirm who can authorize it, and update every affected handoff. A message received is not the same as a change completed.

Operations 14 / Sale-day flow

How to Prevent Estate Sale Pricing and Discount Errors

Make the current price rule easy to apply and a conflicting instruction easy to spot. Researching a value and applying an approved price are different tasks.

Operations 15 / Handoffs and closeout

An Estate Sale Pickup System That Reduces Mix-Ups and Missed Collections

Keep payment, scheduling, and physical collection as separate states. The person releasing goods should be able to verify the item and the authorized collection without guessing.

Operations 16 / Handoffs and closeout

How to Plan Estate Sale Leftovers Before They Become a Cleanout Problem

Agree what happens to unsold goods before the sale. A planned destination is not a completed transfer, and leftover property does not automatically belong to the crew.

Measure, investigate, and test

Operations 17 / Learning and tools

Estate Sale KPIs: What to Measure Beyond Gross Sales

Choose measures that explain the work and the promises being kept. Give every rate a clear numerator, denominator, time window, and decision owner.

Operations 18 / Learning and tools

How to Find the Real Cause of Repeated Estate Sale Problems

Record what happened before deciding why. Rank recurring problems, test the proposed explanation, and check whether the corrective action prevents the same failure.

Operations 19 / Learning and tools

Before Buying Estate Sale Software, Map the Work It Needs to Replace

Start with the work that needs to change. Ask a product to demonstrate the whole handoff with fictional records before trusting a feature list or a promised time saving.

Operations 20 / Learning and tools

How to Test an Estate Sale Process Change Without Risking the Whole Sale

Make the change small enough to understand and reversible enough to stop. Define what success means before the trial, including the quality and service that must remain intact.

Business and client guides

01 / Business systems

How to Grow an Estate Sale Business in Texas

Start with the next job your team can deliver well. Then connect your marketing, inquiry process, and calendar to that goal.

02 / Professional relationships

How Estate Sale Companies Build Referral Partnerships

Make it easy for another professional to understand your fit, explain your process, and introduce a client with confidence.

03 / Website clarity

Estate Sale Website Checklist: Turn Visits Into Consultations

Walk through your site as a seller, a shopper, and a professional making an introduction. Check what each person can understand and do.

04 / Seller intake

How to Qualify Estate Sale Leads Before Booking a Consultation

Book a walk-through with a clear purpose. A short first conversation should establish what the family needs, what is still unknown, and whether your team can help.

05 / Owner economics

Is This Estate Sale Worth Taking? A Job-Cost and Minimum-Fee Worksheet

A full calendar does not tell you what each job earns. Put the work, the fee structure, and the downside case on one sheet before quoting.

06 / Customer follow-up

How to Ask for Estate Sale Reviews Without Review Gating

An honest invitation should not depend on whether you expect praise. Set a consistent service milestone, offer the same review route, and keep support open to everyone.

07 / For families

Estate Sale Commissions and Extra Fees: Compare What You Actually Keep

A percentage is one part of the proposal. Put the included work, minimum charges, extra costs, and expected seller proceeds beside one another before deciding.

08 / Closeout records

What Should an Estate Sale Payout Statement Include?

A family should be able to follow the money from the sale records to the payment. Agree the reporting detail in advance, then make every deduction and remaining amount traceable.

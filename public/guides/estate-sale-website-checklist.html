Estate Sale Website Checklist: Turn Visits Into Consultations

Walk through your site as a seller, a shopper, and a professional making an introduction. Check what each person can understand and do.

By Joel PrentissPublished November 8, 2026

In this guide

  1. Give each visitor a clear path
  2. Show verifiable information
  3. Test the inquiry handoff
  4. Check mobile and accessibility
  5. Help professional referrers
  6. Check search discovery
  7. Run the self-assessment

Give each visitor a clear path

An estate sale website has at least two immediate jobs: help someone evaluate your firm, and help someone find a sale. If both paths start with the same vague "Learn more" button, visitors have to work out your structure before they can act.

Use plain labels such as Hire us or Estate sale services for potential seller clients, and Upcoming sales for shoppers. Keep both visible in the main navigation on a phone. Sale listings can link to your chosen marketplace; the service page should explain your own process.

On the seller path, answer the first practical questions: what you handle, where you work, how you assess fit, and what a consultation covers. Describe any consultation charge or conditions accurately. Do not label every visit "free" by default.

On the shopper path, keep dates and status current. Label past sales as past. Make item questions and pickup questions easy to direct to the appropriate sale contact, instead of sending them into a new-client form.

Show information a visitor can verify

State the business name, the people doing the work, actual services, and current contact information. Use real photographs with permission. A stock image can set a mood, but it should not be presented as your team, a client's home, or a completed project.

  • Service areas: name the places you can serve reliably. A Georgetown firm should explain its actual coverage around Sun City, Round Rock, Cedar Park, and Austin instead of claiming every Texas city. Include any scheduling or travel considerations that change the decision.
  • Credentials: give the exact current designation, issuer, and holder where relevant. Explain the scope. Do not use vague "licensed and certified" language that implies a credential you cannot identify.
  • Reviews: preserve the meaning of real feedback and make its source clear. Distinguish a shopper's review from a seller client's experience. Do not invent testimonials or suggest a rating proves results for future clients.
  • Examples: show what the firm actually did, with permission and identifying details removed where necessary. Label sample reports and illustrative situations clearly.

For firms offering appraisals, specify personal property and the relevant item types and assignment scope. A visitor should not mistake the service for a real-estate appraisal, or assume a liquidation estimate is a written appraisal.

Test the handoff after someone asks for help

A good-looking button is only the start. Submit a clearly labeled internal test with your own contact details and follow it through to the person who replies. Check the inbox, assignment, acknowledgment, and next action.

For a first inquiry, ask for enough to route the conversation: name, a workable contact method, town or ZIP code, requested service, approximate timing, and a brief description. If you ask for the person's role in arranging the work, include an option for someone who is still finding out what they need.

Make required fields obvious and explain what happens after submission. Avoid requiring a complete inventory, a document upload, or sensitive estate information just to begin a conversation. Collect necessary details later through your established process.

Example acknowledgment: "Your inquiry has been received. [Name or team] will contact you by [response window you can maintain] to discuss the location, timing, and service needed. A consultation is arranged after that conversation." Use this only if your system can confirm receipt. If it can confirm only that a message was sent, describe that accurately.

Test a missing field, an invalid email, and a failed submission. The page should explain the problem and preserve what the person entered where practical. Provide a working phone or email alternative. Check weekend coverage before promising a fast reply.

Use the intake funnel and inquiry tracker to connect the website request with qualification and consultation. Count confirmed inquiries separately from clicks on a contact button.

Use a phone, a keyboard, and larger text

Open the site on a narrow screen and try both visitor paths. Text should remain readable without horizontal page scrolling. Navigation should open predictably, and contact controls should be easy to distinguish from decoration.

Then work through the page using only Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, and the other keys its controls require. You should be able to see where focus is, operate navigation, and reach and complete the inquiry form. Test at increased text size or browser zoom as well.

  • Give every input a persistent, descriptive label. Placeholder text alone disappears as someone types.
  • Explain errors in words near the relevant field; do not rely only on a red border.
  • Use meaningful link text and a logical heading order.
  • Describe informative images with useful alternative text, and avoid redundant descriptions on decorative images.
  • Make controls sufficiently separated and readable, and avoid placing essential information inside moving graphics.
  • Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep the content usable when animation is unavailable.

The W3C form-label tutorial explains how labels connect to controls. These checks are a practical first pass, not a certification of accessibility or a substitute for a fuller review with assistive technology.

Give professional referrers a useful page

A professional should be able to send one link that answers: who is this company right for, how does a client get started, and who handles coordination?

Include actual service scope and geography, your consultation process, a direct contact, and the documentation you normally provide. Explain how a client can authorize communication with other professionals. Keep legal decisions and appraisal assignments with the appropriate qualified people.

Use logos, names, testimonials, or "preferred provider" language only when accurate and authorized. A list of local attorneys you hope to meet is not a partner page. The referral-partner guide includes a capability-sheet checklist and an introduction template.

Check discovery without promising a ranking

Give important service pages their own useful text, descriptive titles, and clear links from navigation or related pages. A city page should answer a real local question; repeating the same paragraph with a different place name does little to help a visitor decide.

Ask whoever maintains the site to verify that important pages return real content, use the intended canonical URLs, and are not accidentally blocked from crawling or marked noindex. Include the URLs in the sitemap and inspect them in Search Console when access is available.

Google's SEO Starter Guide explains that useful content and technical accessibility help search engines understand pages, while indexing and ranking are not guaranteed. Measure search visibility separately from inquiries and accepted work.

Run the self-assessment

Use your live site and a clearly labeled internal test inquiry. Check a statement only after you have verified it. These boxes are a temporary worksheet, not a saved audit; print the page or record your findings elsewhere before leaving.

What can you demonstrate today?

Turn the unchecked items into a repair list

For each gap, record the page, what failed, the responsible person, and a retest date. Fix broken contact paths and misleading information first. Then address access and navigation barriers, followed by missing explanations and discovery improvements.

Repeat the same task after each fix. The useful outcome is evidence that a visitor can complete the next step, not a decorative score or a promise that every visit will become a client.

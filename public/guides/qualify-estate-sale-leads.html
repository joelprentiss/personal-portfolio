How to Qualify Estate Sale Leads Before Booking a Consultation

Book a walk-through with a clear purpose. A short first conversation should establish what the family needs, what is still unknown, and whether your team can help.

By Joel PrentissPublished November 8, 2026

In this guide

  1. Decide what the first conversation must resolve
  2. Keep the initial form short
  3. Use six questions in the qualification call
  4. Keep unknowns visible instead of guessing
  5. Give every inquiry a clear next step
  6. Use one record, then review your decisions

Decide what the first conversation must resolve

A request to “sell everything” can mean several different jobs. One caller needs a managed household sale. Another wants an appraisal of a collection. A third needs a house empty before a closing and has already removed most of the contents. Treating those requests as identical creates a calendar full of visits without a clear service fit.

Qualification is a routing decision. It is not a valuation, a legal finding, or a promise to accept the sale. Your goal is to decide whether to arrange a consultation, request one missing fact, suggest a different type of help, or decline with a useful explanation.

Before adding questions to a form, write your own operating limits: the locations you serve, the preparation time you need, available crew dates, the work you handle, and the work you refer elsewhere. Use actual capacity. A Georgetown firm should account for repeated trips to a property in Austin, not only whether the first drive fits on a map.

Separate shoppers asking about an advertised sale from families considering hiring you. The business-growth guide covers that broader funnel. Here, the focus is what happens after a potential seller asks for help.

Keep the initial form short

Ask for a name, one reliable contact method, town or ZIP code, requested service, target timing, and a brief description of the situation. Offer an “I’m not sure” service option. Someone coordinating a parent’s move may not know whether they need liquidation, move management, or an appraisal.

Explain who will respond and the response window your team can maintain. Do not require a complete inventory before a person can speak with you. Reserve the full address and detailed access information for the stage that needs them.

Photos can help with an initial discussion, but request a few broad views only when useful, explain how to send them through your established channel, and obtain permission to retain them. Ask callers to keep personal records and sensitive belongings out of view. A public inquiry form should not collect wills, identity documents, account statements, or alarm codes.

Use six questions in the qualification call

Start with: “I’d like to understand the situation so we can decide whether a visit would be useful. We don’t need a complete item list today.” Then work through these questions in a conversational order.

  1. What outcome matters most? Ask whether the priority is selling suitable contents, meeting a move date, emptying particular rooms, or obtaining a personal-property appraisal. Record the caller’s own description instead of converting every request into an estate sale.
  2. Where is the property, and what dates matter? Separate the preferred sale date from the deadline for returning the property. Include time for preparation, the sale, pickup, and agreed closeout. Ask whether an approval or closing date is still uncertain.
  3. Who is coordinating the decision? Ask who owns the contents, who expects to authorize the work, and whether anyone disagrees about what may be sold. Record the caller’s stated role without treating the answer as proof of authority.
  4. What will actually be available? Ask about rooms, broad categories, condition, collections, and items the family intends to keep. “The house is full” is not a usable inventory assumption. A valuable-looking object in a photo may be excluded from the sale.
  5. What affects access and preparation? Discuss occupancy, stairs, parking, utilities, pets, restricted areas, and any known safety or community constraints. Ask what remains unknown. Do not ask a caller to clear or enter an unsafe area to take photographs.
  6. What help is expected after the sale? Clarify whether the family expects pickup coordination, donation, hauling, cleaning, or an empty house. These are separate scope decisions, not automatic consequences of holding a sale.

For a property in Sun City, Georgetown, Round Rock, Cedar Park, or Austin, record the actual address jurisdiction and any community contact that must confirm restrictions. This is a verification task, not a claim that one city’s or neighborhood’s rules apply everywhere.

Keep unknowns visible instead of guessing

The Texas State Law Library’s executor guide explains that being nominated as executor in a will is distinct from court appointment. A caller’s family relationship or job title does not resolve the authority question for your firm. Where ownership or authority is unclear, identify the question for the appropriate lawyer before accepting instructions to sell.

Likewise, an intake photo does not establish an item’s value or authenticate it. If the requested service is an appraisal for a specific purpose, route it to a suitably qualified personal-property appraiser. Do not describe your liquidation estimate as that report.

A family deciding what to retain may still be a suitable prospect. Put the inquiry in a pending state with one concrete next action, such as “confirm the retained furniture list before estimating setup.” Avoid inventing a universal dollar minimum for all estate sales. Test the inspected scope against your own job-cost worksheet.

Give every inquiry a clear next step

Four outcomes for a qualification conversation
RouteWhat to say and record
ConsultationThe apparent fit is workable. Confirm the visit’s purpose, any charge, participants, preparation, and decisions that will follow inspection.
PendingName the missing fact, who will obtain it, and when you will reconnect. Do not hold a production date without explaining the reservation terms.
Different serviceExplain the service category that may fit. Share a provider’s details only as an option whose availability and suitability the family must confirm.
DeclineGive the practical reason, such as your preparation calendar or scope. Avoid making a judgment about the family or declaring the belongings worthless.

Original response example: “Thank you for explaining the move. With the house due back on [date], our current crew schedule does not leave enough preparation time for the service we provide. You may want to ask a move-management or cleanout provider about the remaining work. I can explain the distinction if that would help.”

If an attorney, realtor, or move manager introduced the family, ask the family what may be shared back. A status such as “we connected” may be enough. The referral-partnership guide explains the handoff without assuming a referral agreement.

Use one record, then review your decisions

Download the blank qualification record (CSV)

The record includes contact preference, inquiry source, service requested, location, deadlines, caller role, contents and exclusions, access unknowns, route, next action, responsible person, and follow-up date. Keep sensitive supporting documents in your established protected system, not in a shared lead spreadsheet.

At the end of a consultation, compare what you expected with what you found. Was the deadline different? Had the retained-item list changed? Did the family expect a cleanout your form never mentioned? Improve the question that would have surfaced the difference.

Review declined and pending inquiries as well as accepted jobs. Count seller inquiries, completed qualification calls, consultations, and signed work separately. Use specific reasons such as “outside service area” or “deadline before crew availability,” not a vague “bad lead” category. This tells you whether to clarify the website, change scheduling, or improve the first conversation.
